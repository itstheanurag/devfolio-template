## Introduction

Over the past three years, I’ve worked as a backend engineer building and maintaining production-grade systems in the healthcare and research space. My role went far beyond writing APIs. I was responsible for designing systems, owning workflows end to end, integrating third-party services, and ensuring that what we shipped could survive real-world usage.

This post is a breakdown of the **work-related projects I handled**, the **systems I built**, and the **responsibilities I owned** while working at CognitiveClouds.

---

## Scope of Work

During my time as a Software Engineer at CognitiveClouds (Nov 2022 – Aug 2025), I actively worked on **three major production platforms**, along with several proof-of-concepts and internal modules.

The projects were deployed as consumer-facing healthcare applications and research platforms, each with its own domain complexity, compliance requirements, and operational challenges.

Across these projects, I:

- Designed backend architectures
- Implemented core business workflows
- Integrated external services and APIs
- Worked closely with frontend teams
- Owned features from design to production deployment

---

## Core Responsibilities

My responsibilities consistently fell into a few key areas:

- Designing and implementing REST APIs used by production web and mobile clients
- Building time-sensitive workflows using cron jobs, background workers, and pub-sub patterns
- Integrating third-party services such as payment gateways, healthcare platforms, and analytics tools
- Handling real-time features using WebSockets
- Ensuring reliability through error handling, retries, and observability
- Taking ownership of projects from initial design to deployment

I was often responsible not just for _building_ features, but for ensuring they worked reliably under real usage.

---

## Project 1: Cadence (Research & Telehealth Platform)

Cadence was a research-focused healthcare platform designed to support structured studies and remote patient interaction.

### What I Worked On

I contributed to multiple core modules, including:

- Onboarding flows for participants
- CMS-style configuration modules for research teams
- Scheduling systems for sessions and follow-ups
- Telehealth workflows supporting video calls

Beyond standard CRUD APIs, the platform required **complex, time-driven behavior**. I implemented cron-based workflows to manage reminders, session lifecycles, and participant timelines.

### Notable Integrations

- Video calling infrastructure
- Audio transcription pipelines
- Action scoring systems for research analysis
- BigQuery pipelines for large-scale reporting and analytics

This project required close coordination with analysts and researchers to ensure backend data pipelines aligned with how the data was consumed downstream.

---

## Project 2: SDG (Schweiger) – Therapy Management Platform

The SDG platform focused on supporting multiple therapy programs, including SCIT, SLIT, and OIT.

### System Responsibilities

On this project, I worked on:

- Questionnaire systems for therapy tracking
- Medication and symptom logging workflows
- Notification systems for reminders and alerts
- Real-time chat using WebSockets

### Payments & Automation

A major responsibility was implementing **recurring subscription payments** using the Square API. This involved:

- Handling webhook-driven payment events
- Managing subscription state transitions
- Ensuring idempotency and failure recovery

I also built cron-driven reminders and automation to ensure patients stayed on schedule, which was critical to therapy adherence.

### External Integrations

- Integrated ModMed to sync and consume patient data
- Ensured secure and reliable data exchange between systems

---

## Project 3: Medlinks – Modular Healthcare Backend

Medlinks was a more generalized healthcare backend designed to support multiple roles and data sources.

### Architecture & Access Control

I designed and implemented:

- A modular backend architecture
- Role-based access control (RBAC) for clinicians, participants, and administrators

This ensured that sensitive data was exposed only to the appropriate users.

### Health Data & Aggregation

I worked on integrations with wearable devices to ingest:

- Glucose data
- Weight measurements
- Vital signs

On top of raw ingestion, I built aggregation APIs that allowed clients to query historical health insights in a structured way.

Given the healthcare context, special care was taken around **data handling practices aligned with HIPAA-style requirements**.

## Owning Projects End-to-End

Across these platforms, I frequently:

- Led proof-of-concept initiatives
- Designed system architecture before implementation
- Took features from initial requirements to production deployment
- Acted as a backend point-of-contact for frontend teams

This ownership model meant I wasn’t just implementing tickets. I was responsible for how systems behaved in production and how they evolved over time.

## Closing Thoughts

Working across multiple healthcare and research platforms taught me how backend systems behave under real constraints. Time-sensitive workflows, third-party failures, data compliance, and long-running systems are very different from demo applications.

These projects shaped how I think about backend engineering today: design for failure, own systems end to end, and treat production as a first-class concern.
